- Let $A$ bve an abelian surface, $\iota:(x,y)\mapsto(-x,-y)$ be the involution, $A[2]$ be the $16$ fixed points, $B=A \setminus A[2]$, $\tilde A$ be the blow-up of $A$ at the $16$ points, and $X=\tilde A/\iota$ is the Kummer surface.
- On $B/\iota$, we choose some flat bundle $V$. Note $\pi_1(B/\iota) = \Z^4 \rtimes \Z_2$.
- Then we extend it to a bundle on $X$ by gluing with a bundle from the Eguchi-Hanson space at each exceptional divisor. A small open set $U$ in $B$ around a (removed) fixed point has fundamental group $\Z_2$, so our flat bundle splits into a sum of two line bundles. We glue them each with a line bundle from the Eguchi-Hanson space $Y=O_{\mathbb P^1}(-2)$. Since $Pic(Y)=\Z \to Pic(U) = \Z/2$ is the quotient homomorphism, is surjective, so this is always possible. Observe that there are many choices for the two line bundles, characterized by two integral multiples of the exceptional divisor, as long as the parities match the flat bundle $V$'s "twisting".
- Because $V$ is flat, it contributes zero to the bulk curvature, meaning the first Chern class of the glued bundle is supported entirely on the exceptional divisors $E_i$. By evaluating the local line bundle choices $\mathcal{O}(m_{i,1}E_i) \oplus \mathcal{O}(m_{i,2}E_i)$ at each Eguchi-Hanson neighborhood, we find that $c_1$ globally evaluates to the integral linear combination $\sum_{i=1}^{16} (m_{i,1} + m_{i,2})[E_i]$.
- We have an ample divisor on $X$ of the form $H = n\pi^*M - \sum_{i=1}^{16} E_i$, where $M$ is an ample divisor on $A/\iota$. Because $c_1(\mathcal{E})$ is orthogonal to $\pi^*M$, the degree $\deg_H(\mathcal{E})$ and slope $\mu(\mathcal{E})$ are determined exclusively by the exceptional divisors. This yields a slope of $\mu(\mathcal{E}) = \sum_{i=1}^{16} (m_{i,1} + m_{i,2})$.
- The Picard group decomposes as $Pic(X) \cong Pic(A) \oplus \Z^{16}$.
- To prove $\mathcal{E}$ is stable, we apply the Generalised Hoppe Criterion: if $H^0(X, \mathcal{E} \otimes \mathcal{L}) = 0$ for all line bundles $\mathcal{L} \in Pic(X)$ with $\deg_H(\mathcal{L}) \leq -\mu(\mathcal{E})$, then $\mathcal{E}$ is stable.
