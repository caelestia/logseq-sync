- Here's my understanding of the definition of birational maps: inclusions of (dense) open subsets are birational, and isomorphisms are obviously birational. All birational maps are generated by these, if we allow the first class to be inverted.
- Here's why:
- **Claim.** Let $X,Y$ be integral schemes locally of finite type over some base $S$. Let $f:X \to Y$ be a morphism inducing an isomorphism $\phi: K(Y) \to K(X)$. Then there are non-empty open subsets $U \subset X$ and $V \subset Y$ such that $U \xrightarrow{\;\simeq\;} V$ via $f$.
- ## Proof
- ### Step 1
- Suppose $X=\Spec B$, $Y=\Spec A$. Then $\phi:A \hookrightarrow B$ is injective and of finite type.
- Let $B=A[b_1,\cdots,b_n]$. Then $b_i \in \Frac B = \Frac A$, so we can write $b_i=a_i/s_i$ where $a_i,s_i\in A$.
- Put $s=s_1\cdots s_n$. Clearly, $A_s \simeq B_s$.
