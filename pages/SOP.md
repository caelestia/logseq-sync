## UROP {{renderer :wordcount_6af0317c-e2a1-4b4d-9587-20d898c9b082}}
	- During my summer research project, I repeatedly wrote down two-dimensional representations which formed what seemed to be an incomplete family. These described an open part with a natural manifold structure, but sixteen points are missing. When I examined limits of these representations, a second class emerged: the type-II parameters supplied the sixteen exceptional divisors, and completed a parameter space which is now a highly interesting compact manifold (the dual Kummer surface). Until then, I had encountered moduli merely as an abstract idea. Here, the geometry of a moduli space arose from classifying explicit objects and understanding how their deformations fit together. This surprising and pleasant realization drew me toward moduli of sheaves on K3 surfaces and abelian varieties.
	- This project, supervised by Dr. Daniel Platt, began with the objective of constructing rank-two bundles on a Kummer surface from flat bundles and proving their slope stability. Because this was my first encounter with Kummer geometry, I began by calculating the canonical bundle and studying polarizations, while Dr. Platt guided me through the background on blow-ups and descending bundles. We developed the gluing construction jointly. I classified the relevant representations and made several choices leading to the final family; the two classes of representations produced the type-I and type-II bundles through the same gluing procedure. When I asked how to understand the resulting bundle without relying on local coordinates, Dr. Platt suggested computing its Chern classes. Carrying out that calculation gave me a concrete global picture of the bundles and the footing I needed to tackle the hardest stage of the project.
	- Proving stability was the most difficult part. I first tried to confine possible destabilizing line bundles to a finite region of the Picard lattice. In light of the Hodge index theorem, I sought bounds on their self-intersection and on their intersection with the chosen polarization. I could not obtain the required upper bound, and trying to realize the polarization geometrically as a curve did not resolve the problem. After I explained these attempts, Dr. Platt suggested replacing the intersection theory with a holonomy argument to further exploit the "rigidity" of the flat bundle. We figured that making this idea work required a precise extension lemma, which I successfully formulated and proved. The resulting vanishing was stronger than the bound I had sought and eliminated the candidate region entirely. We thereby proved that both types of bundles are slope-stable for a suitable polarization.
	- The moduli conclusion was not part of our initial objective. Once I proved that the type-I family was stable except for the sixteen two-torsion ones, this behavior caught my attention and I thought about the then unused type-II representations. I made a careful limit argument, showing the stable bundles actually assemble into a nice family, thus identifying (components of) the algebraic moduli space with the dual Kummer surface. I completed the final technical proofs and wrote the manuscript "A Family of Stable Rank 2 Bundles on a Kummer Surface" which I have submitted to arXiv. The project made my explicit construction, stability, degeneration, and compactification feel like connected aspects of a single geometric question.
- ## Talk and reading {{renderer :wordcount_6b06a00e-7d14-4fe7-90aa-696e443e7da1}}
	- Alongside this research, I have pursued the sheaf-theoretic and categorical language in geometry. For a one-hour Imperial Mathematics Student Colloquium talk, “From Poincaré Duality to Verdier Duality,” I independently studied material from Kashiwara--Schapira. I initially planned to emphasize a proof sketch and present Alexander duality, a topological application, as a corollary. Instead, I reorganized the exposition around concrete computations with intuition, introducing six-functor and recollement ideas gradually. Discussions with Professor Alessio Corti clarified the relation among the distinguished triangle, local cohomology, and mapping cones, while a conversation with a friend helped me compare what is classically realized with explicit differential forms with the data abstractly retained by the derived framework.
	- Several earlier readings and presentations prepared me for these projects. Auditing the course Complex Manifolds introduced me to Kähler geometry and Bochner techniques, which later helped me understand the holonomy argument in the UROP. My background in algebraic geometry greatly benefited from the lectures I gave in the Hartshorne reading group. In particular, I gave a lecture on spectral sequences, ending with the Hodge-de Rham result; this gave me a useful starting point for my current M3R project with Professor Corti. The project is still at an early stage. I am studying mixed Hodge structures through concrete examples, beginning with punctured curves and a reducible curve assembled from copies of projective lines. I was pleased to see that sheaf theory and Verdier duality, which I had learned for the talk, already play an important role in these computations.
- ## Future research, Berkeley {{renderer :wordcount_6b07454b-7db9-4f29-bdeb-262f5ffe3d47}}
	- My primary research interest for a PhD is in moduli problems in algebraic geometry. My UROP left me wanting to learn the general machinery used to construct and compactify moduli spaces, while my Verdier duality talk and M3R project have drawn me toward understanding what derived and sheaf-theoretic invariants retain about these spaces. In graduate school, I want to build a solid foundation in both moduli theory and categorical methods, and work at their intersection.
	- At Berkeley, I am particularly interested in Professor Martin Olsson's work on moduli spaces, algebraic stacks, and derived categories. His recent paper with Rachel Webb constructs moduli spaces of twisted stable maps from curves whose marked points are allowed to collide, and studies the contractions for their stabilization. I would like to understand how logarithmic and stacky structures record such collisions and contractions in families, and how these tools apply more broadly to moduli problems. I am also interested in his work on derived equivalences over a base, as I want to understand how the derived category of coherent sheaves behaves in families.
	- Alongside this, Professor Vivek Shende's work on the microlocal Riemann-Hilbert correspondence on complex contact manifolds appeals to my interest in seeing how far the classical theory of perverse sheaves can be generalized. In particular, his recent paper relating moduli of rank-two local systems on the projective line to coherent sheaves on the resolution of a corresponding moduli space brings together the exact two directions—explicit moduli geometry and sheaf-theoretic methods—that I hope to pursue. Given how much I have gained from reading groups and student seminars at Imperial, I look forward to joining Berkeley's algebraic geometry group and participating in the Commutative Algebra and Algebraic Geometry Seminar.
