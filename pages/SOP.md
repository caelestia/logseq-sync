- ## UROP {{renderer :wordcount_6af0317c-e2a1-4b4d-9587-20d898c9b082}}
	- During my summer research project, I repeatedly wrote down two-dimensional representations which formed what seemed to be an incomplete family. These described an open part with a natural manifold structure, but sixteen points are missing. When I examined limits of these representations, a second class emerged: the type-II parameters supplied the sixteen exceptional divisors, and completed a parameter space which is now a highly interesting compact manifold (the dual Kummer surface). Until then, I had encountered moduli merely as an abstract organizing idea. Here, the geometry of a moduli space arose from classifying explicit objects and understanding how their deformations fit together. This surprising realization drew me toward moduli of sheaves on K3 and abelian varieties.
	- This project, supervised by Dr. Daniel Platt, began with the objective of constructing rank-two bundles on a Kummer surface from flat bundles and proving their slope stability. Because this was my first encounter with Kummer geometry, I began by calculating the canonical bundle and studying polarizations, while Dr. Platt guided me through the background on blow-ups and descending bundles. We developed the gluing construction jointly: the local splitting of the flat bundles allowed us to glue sums of line bundles across the exceptional divisors. I classified the relevant representations and made several choices leading to the final family; the two classes of representations produced the type-I and type-II bundles through the same gluing procedure. When I asked how to understand the resulting bundle without relying on local coordinates, Dr. Platt suggested computing its Chern classes. The calculation I carried out gave me a useful picture of the construction and was inspiring for the next, hardest stage of the project.
	- Proving stability was the most difficult part. I first tried to confine possible destabilizing line bundles to a finite region of the Picard lattice. In light of the Hodge index theorem, I sought bounds on their self-intersection and on their intersection with the chosen polarization. I could not obtain the required upper bound, and trying to realize the polarization geometrically as a curve did not resolve the problem. After I explained these attempts, Dr. Platt suggested replacing the intersection theory with a holonomy argument to further exploit the "rigidity" of the flat bundle. We figured that making this idea work required a precise extension lemma, which I successfully formulated and proved. The resulting vanishing was stronger than the bound I had sought and eliminated the candidate region entirely. We thereby proved that both types of bundles are slope-stable for a suitable polarization.
	- Reflecting on this experience, I'm reminding myself that a part of the research judgement I want to have is about recognizing the central structures when futher pushing a technical argument seems unnatural.
	- The moduli conclusion was not part of our initial objective. Once I proved that the type-I family was stable except for the sixteen two-torsion ones, this behavior caught my attention and I thought about the then unused type-II representations. I made a careful limiting argument, showing the stable bundles actually assemble into a nice family parametrized by the dual Kummer surface. This identifies certain components of the algebraic moduli space with that surface. I completed the final technical proofs and wrote the manuscript "A Family of Stable Rank 2 Bundles on a Kummer Surface" which I have submitted to arXiv. The project moved me from learning established theories to working on an open-ended problem whose strategy was not supplied in advance. It also made explicit construction, stability, degeneration, and compactification feel like connected aspects of a single geometric question.
- ## Talk and reading {{renderer :wordcount_6b06a00e-7d14-4fe7-90aa-696e443e7da1}}
	- Alongside this research, I have pursued the sheaf-theoretic and categorical language in geometry. For a one-hour Imperial Mathematics Student Colloquium talk, “From Poincaré Duality to Verdier Duality,” I independently studied material from Kashiwara--Schapira as well as the necessary derived categories. I initially planned to emphasize a proof sketch and present Alexander duality, a topological application, as a corollary. Instead, I reorganized the exposition around concrete computations with intuition, introducing six-functor and recollement ideas gradually. Discussions with Professor Alessio Corti clarified the relation among the distinguished triangle, local cohomology, and mapping cones, while a conversation with a friend helped me compare what is gained with explicit differential forms in the classical language, with the data abstractly retained in the derived framework. Preparing this expository talk strengthened my ability to select and synthesize difficult material independently.
	- Several earlier readings and presentations prepared me for these projects. Auditing the course Complex Manifolds introduced me to Kähler geometry and Bochner techniques, which later helped me understand the holonomy argument in the UROP. In the Hartshorne reading group, I presented the material on divisors. I also gave a lecture on spectral sequences, ending with the Hodge-de Rham result; this gave me a useful starting point for my current M3R project with Professor Corti. The project is still at an early stage. I am studying mixed Hodge structures through concrete examples, beginning with punctured curves and a reducible curve assembled from copies of projective lines. I was pleased to see that sheaf theory and Verdier duality, which I had learned for the talk, already play an important role in these computations.
- ## Future research {{renderer :wordcount_6b07454b-7db9-4f29-bdeb-262f5ffe3d47}}
	- My main interest for doctoral study is in moduli problems in algebraic geometry. My UROP made me curious about the general methods used to construct and compactify moduli spaces by understanding how limiting objects are added. The talk and M3R have also made me want to understand what derived or other categories remember about these spaces. I hope to build a stronger foundation in moduli theory, algebraic stacks, and categorical methods, and to study how they organize geometric objects in families. I want to learn these methods and use them on problems where the correct compactification or categorical structure is not known in advance.
