- ## UROP {{renderer :wordcount_6af0317c-e2a1-4b4d-9587-20d898c9b082}}
	- During my summer research project, I repeatedly wrote down two-dimensional representations which formed what seemed to be an incomplete family. These described an open part with a natural manifold structure, but sixteen points are missing. When I examined limits of these representations, a second class emerged: the type-II parameters supplied the sixteen exceptional divisors, and completed a parameter space which is now a compact manifold which bears a close relation with the . Until then, I had encountered moduli merely as an abstract organizing idea. Here, the geometry of a moduli space arose from classifying explicit objects and understanding how their deformations fit together. This surprising realization drew me toward moduli of sheaves on K3 and abelian varieties.
	- This project, supervised by Dr. Daniel Platt, began with the objective of constructing rank-two bundles on a Kummer surface from flat bundles and proving their slope stability. Because this was my first encounter with Kummer geometry, I began by calculating the canonical bundle and studying polarizations, while Dr. Platt guided me through the background on blow-ups and descending bundles. We developed the gluing construction jointly: the local splitting of the flat bundles allowed us to glue sums of line bundles across the exceptional divisors. I classified the relevant representations and made several choices leading to the final family; the two classes of representations produced the type-I and type-II bundles through the same gluing procedure. When I asked how to understand the resulting bundle without relying on local coordinates, Dr. Platt suggested computing its Chern classes. The calculation I carried out gave me a useful picture of the construction and was inspiring for the next, hardest stage of the project.
	- Proving stability was the most difficult part. I first tried to confine possible destabilizing line bundles to a finite region of the Picard lattice. In light of the Hodge index theorem, I sought bounds on their self-intersection and on their intersection with the chosen polarization. I could not obtain the required upper bound, and trying to realize the polarization geometrically as a curve did not resolve the problem. After I explained these attempts, Dr. Platt suggested replacing the intersection theory with a holonomy argument to further exploit the "rigidity" of the flat bundle. We figured that making this idea work required a precise extension lemma, which I successfully formulated and proved. The resulting vanishing was stronger than the bound I had sought and eliminated the candidate region entirely. We thereby proved that both types of bundles are slope-stable for a suitable polarization.
	- Reflecting on this experience, I'm reminding myself that a part of the research judgement I want to have is about recognizing the central structures when futher pushing a technical argument seems unnatural.
	- The moduli conclusion was not part of our initial objective. Once I proved that the type-I family was stable except for the sixteen two-torsion ones, this behavior caught my attention and I thought about the type-II ones. I made a careful limiting argument, showing the stable bundles actually assemble into a family parametrized by the dual Kummer surface. In the extremal case, this identifies certain components of the algebraic moduli space with that surface. I completed the final technical proofs and wrote the sole-authored manuscript, which I have submitted to arXiv. The project moved me from learning established theories to working on an open-ended problem whose strategy was not supplied in advance. It also made explicit construction, stability, degeneration, and compactification feel like connected aspects of a single geometric question.
