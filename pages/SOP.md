- ## UROP {{renderer :wordcount_6af0317c-e2a1-4b4d-9587-20d898c9b082}}
	- During my summer research project, I repeatedly wrote down two-dimensional representations whose associated bundles formed what seemed to be an incomplete family. The type-I parameters described an open part, but the geometry corresponding to sixteen exceptional curves was absent. When I examined limits of these representations, a second class emerged: the type-II parameters supplied the sixteen exceptional divisors and completed a parameter space that was itself a dual Kummer surface. Until then, I had encountered moduli merely as an abstract organizing idea. Here, the geometry of a moduli space arose from classifying explicit objects and understanding how their deformations fit together. This surprising realization drew me toward moduli of sheaves on K3 and abelian varieties.
	- This project, supervised by Dr. Daniel Platt, began with the objective of constructing rank-two bundles on a Kummer surface from flat bundles on the complement of the exceptional curves and proving their slope stability. Because this was my first sustained encounter with Kummer geometry, I began by calculating the canonical bundle and studying polarizations, while Dr. Platt guided me through the background on blow-ups and descending bundles. We developed the gluing setup jointly: the local splitting of the flat bundles allowed us to glue sums of line bundles across the exceptional divisors. We also jointly reduced stability to ruling out possible destabilizing line bundles. I classified the relevant two-dimensional representations and made several choices needed to carry out the construction; the two classes of representations produced the type-I and type-II bundles through the same gluing procedure. When I asked how to understand the resulting bundle globally rather than through coordinates, Dr. Platt suggested computing its Chern classes. The Chern–Weil calculation I carried out gave me a more useful global picture of the construction.
