- Exercise 1.1.5.
	- Recall that $\mathrm{Hom}(X,\operatorname{Spec}R)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}(R,\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_X))$. Using this we see that
	- $\mathbf{G}_a=\operatorname{Spec}\Z[X]$, $\mathrm{GL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}\Z[X_{ij}]_{\det(X_{ij})}$, $\mu_m=\operatorname{Spec}{\Z(X)}/{(X^m-1)}$.
	- Regarding the group operations, we can find them using Yoneda. Here I write down the $\mathrm{GL}_n$ example:
		- $m:\mathrm{GL}_n \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_n \otimes_\Z \mathrm{GL}_n$ is given by $X_{ij} \mapsto \sum_\alpha Y_{i\alpha} Z_{\alpha j}$.
		- $e:\mathrm{GL}_n \longrightarrow \Z$ corresponds to the identity matrix, i.e. $X_{ij}\mapsto\delta_{ij}$.
		- $i:\mathrm{GL}_n \longrightarrow \mathrm{GL}_n$ is the inversion, i.e. $X_{ij} \mapsto [(Y_{\alpha\beta})^{-1}]_{ij}$.
	- Finally the constant $\Z$-group associated to a finite group $G$ is given by
	- $G_\Z = \coprod_{g\in G}\operatorname{Spec}\Z$, where the multiplication is given by commuting the (finite!) coproducts with the fiber product and then mapping the $(g,h)$ component to the $gh$ component.
- Exercise 1.1.9.
	- View the tangent space as $T_eG := \big[G(k[\varepsilon])\overset{\,\varepsilon\mapsto0\,}{\longrightarrow} G(k)\big]^{-1}e$, where $k[\varepsilon]:=k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$.
	- The map $\varphi:= dm_{(e,e)}$ is $k$-linear, so $\varphi$ commutes with [the addition]([[Zariski tangent space]]) on $T_eG$.
	- We get the result by the Eckmann--Hilton argument. Since $(T_eG,+)$ is already a monoid, it remains to show that $\varphi$ admits a unit.
	- Using the above definition, $\varphi$ is induced by $m_{k[\varepsilon]}:(G\times G)(k[\varepsilon]) \longrightarrow G(k[\varepsilon])$.
	- The zero tangent vector at $e$ is exactly the image $\bar e$ of $e$ under $G(k)\to G(k[\varepsilon])$.
	- Thus the identity $m_{k[\varepsilon]}(\bar e, \mathrm{id}) = \mathrm{id} = m_{k[\varepsilon]}(\mathrm{id}, \bar e)$ on $G(k[\varepsilon])$ shows that $\bar e$ is an identity for $dm_{(e,e)}$ as desired.

- Exercise 1.2.4. It suffices to show that the period map $H_1(C,\R) \to \Omega^1(C)^\vee$ is injective, and the result follows from comparing dimensions. By Hodge decomosition $H^1(C,\mathbb C)=\Omega^1(C) \oplus \overline{\Omega^1(C)}$, every closed $1$-form can be (uniquely in this case) written as the sum of a holomorphic $1$-form $\alpha$ and an antiholomorphic $1$-form $\beta$. Therefore, if a *real* cycle $\gamma$ goes to $0$ under the period map, then $\int_\gamma\alpha=0$ and $\int_\gamma\bar\beta=\overline{\int_\gamma\beta}=0$. However, by Poincare duality, the integral pairing between $H_1(C,\R)$ and $H^1(C,\mathbb C)$ is non-degenerate. Thus $\gamma=0$.
