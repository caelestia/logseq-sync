- Let $\Omega^1(C)$ be the vector space of holomorphic $1$-forms. Then $H^{1,0}\simeq\Omega^1(C)$ and $H^{0,1}\simeq\overline{\Omega^1(C)}$.
- Exercise 1.2.4. It suffices to show that the period map $H_1(C,\R) \to \Omega^1(C)^\vee$ is injective, and the result follows from comparing dimensions. By Hodge decomosition $H^1(C,\mathbb C)=\Omega^1(C) \oplus \overline{\Omega^1(C)}$, every closed $1$-form can be (uniquely in this case) written as the sum of a holomorphic $1$-form $\alpha$ and an antiholomorphic $1$-form $\beta$. Therefore, if a *real* cycle $\gamma$ goes to $0$ under the period map, then $\int_\gamma\alpha=0$ and $\int_\gamma\bar\beta=\overline{\int_\gamma\beta}=0$. However, by Poincare duality, the integral pairing between $H_1(C,\R)$ and $H^1(C,\mathbb C)$ is non-degenerate. Thus $\gamma=0$.
- Let $A:C \longrightarrow J_C := \Omega^1(C)^\vee / H_1(C,\Z)$ be the Abel--Jacobi map (for some fixed base point $p$).
- Alternate description of the Jacobian.
	- Expontial sheaf sequence (with the $\exp(2\pi i-)$ convention) gives $\mathrm{Pic}^0(C)=H^{0,1}(C)/H^1(C,\Z)$. We now show that there is an isomorphism $\mathrm{Pic}^0(C) \to J_C$. First, Serre duality gives the isomorphism between the vector spaces:
	- $$\Phi:H^{0,1} \to (H^{1,0})^\vee,\qquad \eta \mapsto \big(\omega \mapsto \int_C \eta\wedge\omega\big)$$
	- It remains to find the image of $H^1(C,\Z)$.
	- The cohomology map $H^1_{dR}(C,\mathbb C)\to H^{0,1}(C)$ induced by sheaf inclusion $\underline{\mathbb C} \to \mathcal{O}_C$ is precisely the Hodge projection to $(0,1)$-classes.
	- By [the comparison between sheaf cohomology and singular cohomology]([[Cohomology of constant sheaf and singular cohomology]]), and the UCT, the image of $H^1(C,\underline\Z) \to H^1(C,\underline{\mathbb C})$ is the subset of cohomology classes that are integral valued on $H_1(C,\Z)$.
	- Let $\gamma\in H_1(C,\Z)$ be a cycle, and $\eta$ be its Poincare dual defined by $\int_C\eta\wedge\omega=\int_\gamma \omega$ for all $\omega$. Then $\Phi(\eta^{0,1})=\int_\gamma$ under the above isomorphism, as desired. Since $\eta\in H^1(C,\Z) \subset H^1_{dR}(C,\R)$, $\eta^{0,1}$ uniquely determines $\eta$ as desired.
- Abel's theorem.
	- Define another map $B:C \longrightarrow \mathrm{Pic}^0(C)$ as $B(x)=[\mathcal{O}_C(x-p)]$.
	- Theorem: $A=B$ under the above identification of the codomain.

- Corollary. The Abel--Jacobi map $A$ is injective.
	- Proof. If $D$ is any divisor of degree $0$, and $A(D)=0$, then $B(D)=[\mathcal{O}(D)]=0$, i.e. $D$ is principal. If $A(p)=A(q)$, this implies the existence of a meromorphic function on $C$ with exactly one simple pole. This means $C$ is rational, a contradiction.
- Theorem. The Abel--Jacobi map $A$ is a closed embedding with a smooth image.
	- Proof. The derivative is given by $dA(v)(\omega)=\omega_x(v)$ for all $v\in T_xC$. Therefore it suffices to prove that, for any $x\in C$, there is some $\omega\in\Omega^1(C)$ such that $\omega_x\neq0$, in other words, that $|K_C|$ is basepoint-free. By Riemann--Roch,
	- $$\ell(p)-\ell(K_C-p)=\deg(p)-g+1=2-g.$$
	- By the same reason as the above proof, $C$ is not rational implies $\ell(p)=1$. Thus
	- $$\ell(K_C-p)=g-1 < \ell(K_C).$$
	- The result follows.

- A complex torus is a connected compact Lie group over $\mathbb C$.
- Theorem 1.2.8. Every complex torus $A$ is commutative and the holomorphic exponential map $\exp:T_0A \to A$ is a surjective homomorphism with kernel a full lattice $\Lambda$.
	- Proof. If $g\in G$, put $c_g:G\to G$, $a\mapsto gag^{-1}$. Let $\mathfrak{g}=T_eG$. The adjoint representation is defined as $\mathrm{Ad}:G \to \mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})$, $g \mapsto [dc_g]_e$.
	- Since $G=A$ is connected compact, the maximum modulus principle forces $\mathrm{Ad} \equiv \mathrm{Ad}(e) = \mathrm{id}$ to be constant.
	- Lemma. A homomorphism between connected Lie groups is uniquely determined by the derivative at $e$.
		- [Proof]([[Lie group homomorphism is determined by derivative at identity]])
	- This implies $c_g=\mathrm{id}$ for all $g$, so $A$ is commutative and $\exp$ is a homomorphism. Its image is then an open subgroup of $A$, which must be all of $A$ by connectedness. Since the derivative at $0$ is an isomorphism, the kernel is discrete. Thus the kernel must be a full lattice. QED
